1 · What each family asked for

Calls per business per day, from the connectors survey

18 answers from 2 November. Each family picked a band per connector, so the bars show the lowest and highest the bands allow, summed over that family's connectors. The scale is logarithmic so the small families stay visible.

Bands: "under 10" counted as up to 10, "10 to 100" and "100 to 1,000" as written. Total per business: 160 to 1,710 a day. Many of OF3's calls go to Google, WordPress or other hosts, not Zernio or Composio, so this is the whole picture, not one provider's share.

2 · The multiplier

One inbox check is up to 26 Zernio requests

The inbox asks every connected channel for new messages every 30 seconds while the page is open. Reading comments through Zernio takes one request to find the account, one to list posts, then one per post that has comments, for up to 10 posts.

WhatsApp arrives by Meta's own push to Swayu, so it costs no reads. A second open tab doubles the count, because reads are shared only while one is already in flight.

3 · Where it breaks

Zernio requests per minute as businesses open their inbox

Busiest case: every channel has 10 recent posts with comments. Measured on the test business on 5 November: about 570 reads over 12 minutes, one every 5 seconds, with 0 failures and 0 limit answers, because the test posts have few comments. The busiest case has not been measured.

5 · Running at the same time

What already holds, and the gap

Holds today, inside one running app

One budget per provider, shared by every lead and every open tab in that app. Taking a slot has no gap between the check and the take, so two leads cannot both take the last slot.

A 429 pauses every call to that provider until the wait is over. A caller that would wait more than 10 seconds fails at once with the reason.

Ten leads asking for the same read at the same moment cost one read. Replies are never shared.

Each answer's limit header raises or lowers the budget to the provider's real number.

The gap: Zernio counts the team, the budget counts one app

Two ways to close it. One shared counter in the database that every app takes from. Or one gateway service that every family calls, so one budget sees every request.
