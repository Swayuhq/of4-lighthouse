Question 2Why did it check every 30 seconds?

A laptop has no public address, so Zernio could not push to it. Asking was the only way in, until push was built. Then the timer should have gone the same day.

Question 3Without the timer, how does a customer's message come in?

seen live

Zernio pushes it to us the moment it gets it. We check the signature, save it once, and write a draft.

Most of the wait is Facebook reaching Zernio, which we do not control. Our part is about 1 to 2 seconds. If a push fails, Zernio retries it 7 times over about 51 hours.

Question 4Which limit, exactly?

built

Each provider's request budget per minute, for all of Swayu. One shared counter per provider. When a provider says "too many", every call waits.

Owner: the connector layer.

Question 5Can 1,000 lead agents use one connector at the same time?

simulated

Reading, yes. Replying, not yet: 400 of 1,000 are refused today. Replies waiting in line fixes that.

8 lead agents per business, so 1,000 lead agents are 125 businesses. At 20 per business, refusals start above 30 businesses. The budget is 600 replies a minute for all of Swayu either way.

Question 6How many per second?

seen live

Replies: 10 a second for all of Swayu on Zernio's paid tier, 1 on the free tier. That is Zernio's ceiling, not our code. Reads are not the bottleneck.

Question 7How did you conclude concurrency was fine?

simulated

Before the call, from watching 4 agents. That proved nothing. Now from our real gateway and rate limiter, run at four sizes on a simulated clock.

Not tested: 1,000 real requests against Zernio itself, because that spends the real budget.
